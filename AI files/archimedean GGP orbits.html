Conventions

  • \(S_3(\mathbb R)=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb C):\gamma\bar\gamma=1\}\). An element \(h\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb R)\) acts by conjugation by \(\mathrm{diag}(h,1)\). The vector \(e_0\) is the last basis vector, and \(W=\mathrm{span}(e_1,e_2)\).
  • \(\mathrm{inv}(\gamma)=(\det\gamma,\operatorname{tr}\gamma,b)\) with \(b=e_0^t\gamma e_0\). Elements with the same \(\mathrm{inv}\) form one fibre.
  • An element is semisimple if its orbit is closed and regular if its stabilizer is trivial. Both properties belong to the whole orbit. Regular semisimple means both, which is \(\Delta_S\ne0\).
  • \(K_\gamma=[e_0,\gamma e_0,\gamma^2e_0]\) and \(L_\gamma=[e_0,\gamma^te_0,(\gamma^t)^2e_0]^t\), and \(\Delta_S=\det K_\gamma\det L_\gamma/(\det\gamma)^2\).
  • An arrow \(A\to B\) means that \(B\) lies in the closure of \(A\). Conjugating by \(\mathrm{diag}(h_1,h_2,1)\) multiplies \(\gamma_{ij}\) by \(h_i/h_j\) (with \(h_3=1\)).
  • Every matrix was checked numerically: \(\gamma\bar\gamma=1\), the common \(\mathrm{inv}\), the stabilizer dimension, \(\det K_\gamma\), \(\det L_\gamma\), and the limit along each arrow.

The orbit space in three dimensions

\(\mathbb O(S_3(\mathbb R))=\mathrm U(1)\times\mathbb C^2\) has real dimension five. To draw it, replace it by its real analogue \(\{1\}\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb C\), the slice

\[\Sigma=\{\det\gamma=1,\ \operatorname{tr}\gamma\in\mathbb R\},\]

with coordinates \(h=\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}\gamma-1)\) and \(b=e_0^t\gamma e_0=x+iy\). Fixing \(\det\gamma=1\) loses nothing. Multiplying \(\gamma\) by a scalar \(\zeta\in\mathrm U(1)\) commutes with \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb R)\), sends \(\mathrm{inv}=(d,c,b)\) to \((\zeta^3d,\zeta c,\zeta b)\), and preserves \(\Delta_S\) and the chambers. On \(\Sigma\) the characteristic polynomial is \((X-1)(X^2-2hX+1)\). So every \(\gamma\) has the eigenvalues \(1,\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\): \(\lambda=e^{i\theta}\) with \(\cos\theta=h\) if \(|h|\le1\), and \(\lambda=h+\sqrt{h^2-1}\), real, if \(|h|>1\). All eight fibres of the explorer lie in \(\Sigma\). On \(\Sigma\) the discriminant factors as

\[\Delta_S=4\,(x-h)\,\bigl((1-h)\,y^2-(1+h)\,(x-1)^2\bigr).\]
  • The first factor is the plane \(x=h\). There \(e\) is orthogonal to the eigenvector of \(g\) for \(1\).
  • The second factor is a Plücker conoid. There \(e\) is orthogonal to the eigenvector for \(\lambda\) or for \(\lambda^{-1}\). At height \(|h|\le1\) the conoid is the pair of lines from \(b=1\) through \(\lambda\) and through \(\lambda^{-1}\). For \(|h|>1\) only its axis \(b=1\) is left.
  • Away from the corners, \(\mathbb C[g]e\) is a plane on both, so these are the walls.
  • Chambers, for \(|h|<1\): write \(b=\beta_1+\beta_2\lambda+\beta_3\lambda^{-1}\) with \(\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3=1\). Each \(\beta_j\) has the sign of the corresponding eigenline of \(g\). So the number of negative \(\beta_j\) is \(0\), \(1\) or \(2\), in the \(\mathrm U(3)\), \(\mathrm U(2,1)\) or \(\mathrm U(1,2)\) chamber.
  • Hence the closure of the \(\mathrm U(3)\) chamber is a solid. Its section at height \(h\in[-1,1]\) is the triangle with vertices \(1,\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\), the numerical range of \(g\).

The floor of the picture on the left, seen from straight above: \(h\) to the right and \(y=\operatorname{Im}b\) up. Teal and blue curves are walls next to \(\mathrm U(3)\) and next to \(\mathrm U(1,2)\).

How Figure 1 arises. Let \(S=\{x=1-\beta(h)\}\) be a curved sheet. Here \(\beta\) vanishes for \(h\le-1\), is positive on \((-1,0)\), vanishes at \(0\) and is negative after; in the picture \(\beta(h)=2.6\,(1+h)^2(-h)\) on \([-1,0]\). In the coordinates \((h,y)\) on \(S\):

  • For \(h<-1\), \(S\) is the plane \(x=1\). It lies in \(\mathrm U(2,1)\), except for the axis \(y=0\): this is the dashed line (e).
  • At \(h=-1\), \(S\) passes through the full reduced origin \(P_-\).
  • For \(-1<h<0\), \(S\) cuts the \(\mathrm U(3)\) solid in \(|y|<w(h)\), where \(w(h)=|\beta(h)|\sqrt{(1+h)/(1-h)}\). This is the lens, bounded by walls (c). Since \(w\sim1.8\,(1+h)^{5/2}\), the lens has a cusp at \(P_-\).
  • At \(h=0\), \(S\) crosses the axis at the split corner (d), the point of tab (d).
  • For \(h>0\), \(S\) cuts the \(\mathrm U(1,2)\) region at the vertex \(b=1\) in \(|y|<w(h)\). This is the wedge.

So the projection \((h,x,y)\mapsto(h,y)\) along the \(x\)-axis maps \(S\) with its partition onto Figure 1, with \(h\) increasing to the right. The picture draws this literally. Under the slice hangs a copy of \(S\), moved straight down along \(x\), and under the copy lies its flat projection; all three sit at the same \((h,y)\).

At \(P_-\), whose eigenvalues are \(1,-1,-1\), the slice is transverse to the torus of full reduced origins. The conoid \((1-h)y^2=(1+h)(x-1)^2\) is then a Whitney umbrella, as in Figure 2 of the note, and its handle is the nonsplit axis below \(P_-\). At \(P_+\), which is \(\gamma=1\), the slice is tangent to that torus, and the plane \(x=h\) passes through \(P_+\) as well. That is why Figure 1 is drawn at \(P_-\). The fibre of the full reduced origin tab lies over \(P_+\). Translating in the Cayley chart of Lemma 7.8 identifies the fibres over all full reduced origins, so that tab also describes the fibre over \(P_-\).

Conventions

  • \(Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}y+uI_2&v\\w&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_3(\mathbb R)\) with \(y\in\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\), \(v\in\mathbb R^2\), \(w\in(\mathbb R^2)^*\) and \(u,d\in\mathbb R\). An element \(h\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb R)\) acts by conjugation by \(\mathrm{diag}(h,1)\), that is \((y,v,w)\mapsto(hyh^{-1},hv,wh^{-1})\), and \(u,d\) do not change. The vector \(e=e_3\) is the last basis vector, and \(W=\mathrm{span}(e_1,e_2)\). All examples have \(u=d=0\).
  • The invariants are \(u,d\) and \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)=(\det y,\ wv,\ wyv)\). Elements with the same invariants form one fibre, and every value of \((u,d,q)\in\mathbb R^5\) occurs.
  • An element is semisimple if its orbit is closed and regular if its stabilizer is trivial. Both properties belong to the whole orbit. Regular semisimple means both, which is \(\Delta\ne0\).
  • \(K(Y)=[v,yv]\) and \(R(Y)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w\\wy\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Since \(y^2=-q_1\), one has \(R(Y)K(Y)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q_2&q_3\\q_3&-q_1q_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), so \(\det K(Y)\det R(Y)=-\Delta\) with \(\Delta=q_1q_2^2+q_3^2\). Also \(\det[e,Ye,Y^2e]=\det K(Y)\).
  • An arrow \(A\to B\) means that \(B\) lies in the closure of \(A\). Conjugating by \(\mathrm{diag}(h_1,h_2,1)\) multiplies \(Y_{ij}\) by \(h_i/h_j\) (with \(h_3=1\)).
  • Every matrix was checked numerically: the invariants, the stabilizer dimension, \(\det K(Y)\), \(\det R(Y)\), and the limit along each arrow.

Conventions on the unitary side

  • \(V=W\oplus\mathbb Ce\) with \(\langle x,x'\rangle=x'^*J_3x\) and \(J_3=\mathrm{diag}(J,1)\), so \(\langle e,e\rangle=1\) and \(e\perp W\). The unitary Lie side is \(\mathrm{Herm}(J_3)=\{Y:Y^*J_3=J_3Y\}=i\,\mathfrak u(V)\). There \(Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}y+uI_2&v\\v^*J&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(y\) traceless and \(J\)-self-adjoint, \(v\in\mathbb C^2\) and \(u,d\in\mathbb R\). Every representative comes with its own \(J\).
  • \(J\) of signature \((2,0)\), \((1,1)\), \((0,2)\) gives \(\mathrm U(V)=\mathrm U(3)\), \(\mathrm U(2,1)\), \(\mathrm U(1,2)\). The group \(\mathrm U(W)=\{k:k^*Jk=J\}\) acts by conjugation by \(\mathrm{diag}(k,1)\). It is \(\mathrm U(2)\), which is compact, for \(\mathrm U(3)\) and \(\mathrm U(1,2)\), and \(\mathrm U(1,1)\) for \(\mathrm U(2,1)\).
  • The invariants are the same: \(u\), \(d\), \(q_1=\det y\), \(q_2=\langle v,v\rangle\) and \(q_3=\langle yv,v\rangle\), all real. An element of \(M_3(\mathbb R)\) and one of \(\mathrm{Herm}(J_3)\) match when their \((u,d,q)\) agree.
  • The Gram matrix \(K(Y)^*JK(Y)\) of \(v,yv\) is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q_2&q_3\\q_3&-q_1q_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), so \(\Delta=-\det J\,|\det K(Y)|^2\). Hence \(Y\) is regular semisimple exactly when \(e\) is cyclic for \(Y\), and the sign of \(\Delta\) is \(-\operatorname{sign}\det J\): positive for \(\mathrm U(2,1)\), negative for \(\mathrm U(3)\) and \(\mathrm U(1,2)\), where \(q_2=\langle v,v\rangle\) has the sign of \(J\).
  • Weights. If \(q_1=-s^2<0\), then \(y\) has orthogonal eigenlines \(f_1,f_2\) for \(s,-s\), with \(\langle f_j,f_j\rangle=\epsilon_j=\pm1\). For \(v=v_1f_1+v_2f_2\) the weights \(a_j=\epsilon_j|v_j|^2\) satisfy \(q_2=a_1+a_2\) and \(q_3=s(a_1-a_2)\). Their signs are the signs of the eigenlines, and they determine the closed orbit. On the source side the same numbers are \(a_j=w_jv_j\) in an eigenbasis of \(y\).
  • If \(q_1=s^2>0\), the eigenlines of \(y\) for \(\pm is\) are isotropic, so \(J\) has signature \((1,1)\). In a hyperbolic eigenbasis, \(z=\bar v_1v_2\) gives \(q_2=2\,\mathrm{Re}\,z\), \(q_3=2s\,\mathrm{Im}\,z\) and \(\Delta=4s^2|z|^2\).
  • Since \(\mathrm U(W)\) is compact for \(\mathrm U(3)\) and \(\mathrm U(1,2)\), every fibre there is one closed orbit. Non-closed orbits exist only for \(\mathrm U(2,1)\), and only over nonsplit corners and the full reduced origin: these are the points where \(y\) can have an isotropic eigenline, or be nilpotent and nonzero.
  • When \(J\) is hyperbolic, \(k_t=\mathrm{diag}(t,\bar t^{-1},1)\) lies in \(\mathrm U(W)\) for \(t\in\mathbb C^\times\). Conjugating by \(k_t\) multiplies \(Y_{12},Y_{13},Y_{32}\) by \(|t|^2,t,\bar t\), and \(Y_{21},Y_{23},Y_{31}\) by \(|t|^{-2},\bar t^{-1},t^{-1}\).
  • Every matrix was checked numerically: \(Y^*J_3=J_3Y\), the common invariants, the stabilizer dimension in \(\mathrm U(W)\), and the limit along each arrow.

The orbit space in three dimensions

The orbit space \(\mathbb O(M_3(\mathbb R))=\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^3\) has the coordinates \((u,d,q)\). The passive scalars change nothing: translating \(Y\) by \(\mathrm{diag}(uI_2,d)\) commutes with \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb R)\) and \(\mathrm U(W)\), and keeps \(q\), the chambers and the fibres. So it is enough to draw \(\mathbb R^3\) with the coordinates \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)\). There the discriminant is the Whitney umbrella

\[\Delta=q_1q_2^2+q_3^2=0,\qquad\text{that is}\qquad q_3^2=(-q_1)\,q_2^2.\]
  • For \(q_1=-s^2<0\), \(y\) has the eigenvalues \(\pm s\), and the umbrella is the pair of planes \(q_3=\pm s\,q_2\). Away from their crossing these are walls: \(\mathbb C[Y]e\) is a plane. The crossing \(q_2=q_3=0\) is the line of split corners: \(e\) is an eigenvector of \(Y\).
  • For \(q_1=s^2>0\), \(y\) has the eigenvalues \(\pm is\), and only the handle \(q_2=q_3=0\) is left. It consists of nonsplit corners. Around it \(\Delta\ge0\), so only \(\mathrm U(2,1)\) is adjacent.
  • At \(q_1=0\), \(y\) is nilpotent. The two sheets meet tangentially along the fold \(q_3=0\), which consists of walls, and \(q=0\) is the full reduced origin.
  • Chambers: write \(a_{1,2}=\tfrac12(q_2\pm q_3/s)\). These are the weights of \(e\) on the two eigenlines of \(y\): \(q_2=a_1+a_2\), \(q_3=s(a_1-a_2)\) and \(\Delta=-4s^2a_1a_2\). The number of negative \(a_j\) is \(0\), \(1\) or \(2\) in the \(\mathrm U(3)\), \(\mathrm U(2,1)\) or \(\mathrm U(1,2)\) chamber. So \(\mathrm U(3)\) is the trough \(\{|q_3|<s\,q_2\}\), \(\mathrm U(1,2)\) is the trough \(\{|q_3|<-s\,q_2\}\), and \(\mathrm U(2,1)\) is the rest, including every point with \(q_1\ge0\) off the discriminant.
  • The closure of the \(\mathrm U(3)\) chamber is the image of the real symmetric matrices: for them \(a_j=v_j^2\ge0\). Its section at a fixed \(q_1<0\) is an infinite wedge, while on the group page it is a triangle.
  • The picture is a cone. \(Y\mapsto tY\) sends \(q\) to \((t^2q_1,t^2q_2,t^3q_3)\) and \(\Delta\) to \(t^6\Delta\), and it preserves the chambers. So all the sections at \(q_1<0\) look alike after rescaling, and so do all those at \(q_1>0\).

How Figure 1 arises. Let \(S=\{q_2=\beta(q_1)\}\) be a curved sheet. Here \(\beta\) vanishes for \(q_1\ge0\), is positive on \((-1,0)\), vanishes at \(-1\) and is negative after; in the picture \(\beta(q_1)=5\,q_1^2(1+q_1)\) on \([-1,0]\). In the coordinates \((-q_1,q_3)\) on \(S\):

  • For \(q_1>0\), \(S\) is the plane \(q_2=0\). It lies in \(\mathrm U(2,1)\), except for the axis \(q_3=0\): this is the dashed line (e).
  • At \(q_1=0\), \(S\) passes through the full reduced origin.
  • For \(-1<q_1<0\), \(S\) cuts the \(\mathrm U(3)\) trough in \(|q_3|<w(q_1)\), where \(w(q_1)=\sqrt{-q_1}\,\beta(q_1)\). This is the lens, bounded by walls (c). Since \(w\sim5\,(-q_1)^{5/2}\), the lens has a cusp at the origin.
  • At \(q_1=-1\), \(S\) crosses the axis at the split corner (d), the point of tab (d).
  • For \(q_1<-1\), \(S\) cuts the \(\mathrm U(1,2)\) trough in \(|q_3|<\sqrt{-q_1}\,|\beta(q_1)|\). This is the wedge.

So the projection \((q_1,q_2,q_3)\mapsto(-q_1,q_3)\) along the \(q_2\)-axis maps \(S\) with its partition onto Figure 1. On the group page the same figure comes from a sheet through the full reduced origin \(P_-\); the next section explains why the two pictures agree.

The Lie picture inside the group picture

For \(\xi\in\mathrm U(1)\) the Cayley chart \(c_\xi(Y)=\xi(1+iY)(1-iY)^{-1}\) is an equivariant isomorphism from \(\{P>0\}\), where \(P(Y)=|\det(1-iY)|^2\), onto \(U_\xi=\{\gamma:\det(\xi+\gamma)\ne0\}\), and likewise from \(\mathrm{Herm}(J_3)\) to \(\mathrm U(J_3)\) (the note's (C1)). It preserves matching (C3). So it identifies the part of the Lie orbit space where \(P>0\) with the part of \(\mathbb O(S_3(\mathbb R))\) where \(\det(\xi+\gamma)\ne0\), chambers with chambers, strata with strata and fibres with fibres. On eigenvalues it is \(\mu\mapsto\xi\frac{1+i\mu}{1-i\mu}\), which sends the real line to the unit circle.

The table is written for the passive scalars \(u=d=0\); in general the eigenvalues of \(Y|_W\) are \(u\pm s\) or \(u\pm is\). The split corner of the group page, \(\mathrm{diag}(i,-i,1)\), is \(c_1\) of the split corner \(\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)\) here, and \(\gamma=1\) is \(c_1(0)\).

  • One umbrella at every full reduced origin. The plane \(q=0\) of the Lie orbit space, with \(u,d\) free, goes to the torus of full reduced origins \(\mathrm{diag}(\lambda,\lambda,\mu)\) of the group. Near each of them the group picture is this cone. This is why Figure 1 of the note reads the same in both settings.
  • Infinity. Since \(|\det(\xi+c_\xi(Y))|^2=64/P(Y)\), the infinity of the Lie algebra goes to the divisor \(D_\xi=\{\det(\xi+\gamma)=0\}\), and the locus \(P=0\), where \(Y\) has an eigenvalue \(\pm i\), goes to the infinity of the group. So a Schwartz function on the group that vanishes near \(D_\xi\) is a Schwartz function on the Lie algebra that is flat along \(\{P=0\}\). This is where the note needs its flat factor (Lemma 7.52), and four centres \(\xi\in\{\pm1,\pm i\}\) cover the group.
  • The compact chamber. The \(\mathrm U(3)\) trough is not bounded, but \(c_\xi\) sends it to a relatively compact part of the compact \(\mathrm U(3)\) chamber of the group, whose closure meets \(D_\xi\). On the Lie algebra the Gaussian thickening of this chamber is not Schwartz; on the group it is. That is the point of Step 5 and Figure 12 of the note, and why \(f_T\) lives on the group.
